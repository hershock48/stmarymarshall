A letter to the Diocese of Lansing

Seventy-three parishes.
One living platform.
Connected to each other, and to the diocese.

One platform runs every parish’s website and joins the parishes to each other and to the diocese. A Mass time typed once at a parish is on that parish’s site and in one finder for all 73 parishes. The bishop records a message once and it is on every parish home page that hour. The chancery sees every site on one page. The finder for all 73 and the bishop’s voice are running for Lansing today, and three parishes run on the platform in the Diocese of Kalamazoo.

Section two

What changes, and for whom.

For the diocese

One place to look and one place to speak. The chancery sees every site on one page. The bishop records once and is on every parish home page that hour. A notice is typed once and lands everywhere.

For a parish office

Nobody logs into anything. The secretary texts or emails a change and it is up the same week, and a wrong Mass time is fixed the same day. The week’s news is typed once and prints as the bulletin insert.

For someone in the pews

The next Mass near them, from any zip code, at any parish in the diocese. What is on this week two towns over. A parish site that is right on the day the schedule changes.

Section three

What it costs.

The published menu price for a site this size: $2,000 each
No build fee
For every parish under the diocesan license. The migration is one script plus a day of reading per parish, and the first year’s license pays for it.
One line for the diocese. No parish sees a bill.
$4,990/mo
For the diocese, all 73 parishes included, however many move and whenever they do. Hosting, security, backups, the edits, the checks, the feeds, the diocese layer with its chancery view, and people who answer.

That is $59,880 a year, about one percent of this year’s appeal goal, for one platform with every parish on it and everything on it the diocese’s own, against seven vendors’ invoices today. The diocese pays it in one line and no parish ever sees a bill or a per-site number. The diocesan agreement runs twelve months the first year, which is what stands in for a build fee; after that it is month to month, thirty days’ notice, no exit fee. Its terms are the same ones we put in writing for the Diocese of Kalamazoo: read them there ↗, with Lansing’s name and number on a copy the day the diocese asks. A parish that comes on its own, outside the license, pays the published build price and $99 a month.

For the finance office

One invoice a month to the diocese, $4,990, beginning the month after the first piece of the platform is live on an address the diocese or a parish owns. Nothing is billed before that and nothing is ever billed to a parish. There is no build fee, no setup fee and no exit fee. The first term is twelve months; after it either side ends the agreement on thirty days’ notice. The terms are one page in plain words, and what the platform stores and where it runs is another. The Diocesan Services Appeal already lists communications and technology among what it funds.

Giving, stated plainly

Giving through the platform is not built yet. It is the next thing we build, and these are the terms it will run on. A parish that takes gifts through the platform does so on its own Stripe account at the nonprofit rate, 2.2% plus 30 cents a gift, and the platform’s fee is 1% of the gift, capped at $5, which the donor is offered to cover and most do. Nothing is due monthly for giving; a parish that gives little pays little, and a $5,000 memorial gift pays $5. The parish’s donors and their recurring gifts live on the parish’s own account and travel with it. The Diocesan Services Appeal can run on the same rails, on the diocese’s own account, with every parish’s progress on the chancery view. A parish that keeps WeShare, OSV or Vanco keeps it.

What a parish may add, and pays for itself

Hosting its livestream on its own player with automatic recordings and clips, $89 a month. An office panel for self-service edits, $29 a month. Texting at cost plus a margin. Each one a parish chooses; none is required, and the license does not change when one is added. None of the three is running yet. Each is built for the first parish that asks for it, and billed only once it runs.

Who keeps a parish website today

The people who run parish life are in their fifties and sixties; CARA puts the median lay ecclesial minister at 55. The website falls to the secretary or a parishioner who learned one of seven page builders on their own time and holds the only password. We are younger than the people this is being asked of, and we do this for a living. Under the monthly, nobody at any parish logs into anything. The office sends the change by text or email, it is up the same week, and when the secretary retires the website does not notice.

Section four

The part that matters most.

Seventy-three parishes pay seven vendors, and at the end of every month the diocese owns none of it: not the pages, not the photos, not the calendars, and not a schedule of its own parishes.

What the diocese would hold

The diocese owns everything on it, and everything about it.

Glazed Web builds the platform and keeps it running. One codebase, every parish on its own domain with its own photos and words, none of them a template. The diocese owns everything it puts in and everything about it, in writing: every page, photo and document, every schedule, calendar, event and form, its domains in its own registrar account, and, where a parish gives through the platform, a Stripe account in the parish’s own name with every donor and recurring gift in it. Every parish’s schedule is published as structured data and as one diocesan feed, so the finder and Google read it from the diocese instead of from a vendor, and the Mass-finder apps can be fed from the same file. Export at any time, free, as readable files, as data and as a copy of each site that runs on any host; feeds nobody is charged for. Giving through the platform, once it is built, runs at one percent of a gift, capped at $5, with no monthly fee. The diocese also holds the platform itself: a sealed copy, source included, delivered every quarter, with a written license to run it for its own parishes on the terms in the agreement. Nothing of the diocese’s lives only on our server. A twelve-month first term on the diocesan agreement, then month to month, thirty days’ notice, no exit fee.

What support looks like today

For the twenty-six parishes on eCatholic, its terms set customer support at 8 to 5 Central, Monday through Friday, which leaves every Sunday Mass outside it, and say support does not make changes to a parish’s website. Under the license a wrong Mass time is fixed the same day, weekends included, and every other change is made for the office the same week.

Section five

How it starts.

1

We walk you through it

Kevin and Sean come to Lansing with the platform open, or do it by phone, and stay as long as the questions do. Nothing is signed that day.

2

The finder and the bishop’s voice go first

Both are already running. They go live on an address of the diocese’s own, or inside dioceseoflansing.org, for all 73 parishes, before any parish has moved. The diocese’s own website stays as it is.

3

Then the parishes, at their own pace

Name any parish and we build it for the diocese to judge, as St. Philip was built. Each goes live with its pastor’s yes and its office’s corrections, and its old subscription ends after its new site is up, so nothing is ever offline.

4

What is owed, and when

Nothing until the first piece is live on an address of the diocese’s own or a parish’s. From the month after, $4,990 a month for everything, however many parishes have moved. There is no trial that runs out and no fee to leave.

How the parishes would move

Each parish is read from its current site into the platform’s data, a person reads the result once, and the parish office confirms the handful of facts only it knows: holy day Mass times, office hours, which inbox the forms go to. The twenty-six eCatholic sites share one markup and are read by the script already written for Kalamazoo; the others are read the same way, platform by platform. The order is the diocese’s to set. Ours would be the five parishes whose sites do not answer today, then the parishes the diocese names, then the rest as each one’s current agreement allows.

Section six

Parishes connected to each other.

The people of a diocese cannot see each other online. Someone new to a parish has no way to find the young adult night, the men’s group or the parish mission one town over, because each lives on one parish’s calendar, on one of seven platforms, and nowhere else. On the platform those gatherings and groups are on one page for the whole diocese, with a person to call beside each, and it starts with the thing everyone looks for first: the next Mass.

Open dioceseoflansing.org/find-a-parish ↗. It lists the parishes by city, by vicariate and by county, with a map. Ask it when the next Mass is and it cannot say; the directory holds no times, and the site’s own answer is a link to MassTimes.org, a national volunteer directory the diocese does not control and the parishes rarely update. A Catholic in a car in Flint on a Saturday afternoon is on her own.

Every parish has the times, on its own site, in seven different shapes. What is missing is the one place that reads them all, and the only way that place stays right is if the parishes feed it by keeping their own site right. That is what the platform is for: the parish website and the diocesan finder are the same data, typed once.

Today it carries every parish and no times, because the diocese publishes none; the day a parish joins the platform its Mass, Confession and Adoration times appear on its card, worked out from the clock, and a Catholic in a car gets an answer. It re-reads the directory weekly, says on every card when it last did, carries a “wrong time? tell us” link that reaches the chancery, and can be embedded on the diocese’s own site. It goes live first, on an address of the diocese’s own or inside dioceseoflansing.org, before any parish moves.

Section seven

What the diocese sees, and what it sends.

A diocese cannot coordinate what it cannot see, and today it sees seventy-two sites on seven platforms through logins it does not hold. The platform gives the chancery one place to look and one place to speak. What it sends: the bishop’s voice on every parish home page the hour it is posted, a notice written once that lands on every site, and this week across the diocese compiled into one page that is also the weekly email. What it sees: every site’s health and use on one page, by vicariate, with counts and no person in them.

For the communications office

None of this asks the communications office to change a tool. The parish calendars become the submissions for the weekly email: this week across the diocese is one page and one feed, which the office trims and sends from the mail program it already uses. A notice is typed once, with the dates it should run, and is on every parish site that minute. An episode of the bishop’s podcast is listed once. dioceseoflansing.org stays as it is, and the finder can sit inside it.

Running now

The bishop’s voice, on every parish site at once

Bishop Boyea’s Made for Mission podcast is already the first entry: the recording the diocese published, listed once, with a feed anyone can subscribe to in a podcast app, and a card every parish home page on the platform carries. Add a homily, a Lent reflection or a letter read aloud and it is on every site the same hour, in English or Spanish where it exists in both, with no one at any parish copying anything. For the parishes that stream, each gets a Watch page that knows the Mass schedule: the player from a quarter hour before Mass to ninety minutes after, the next Mass in words the rest of the week, so nobody meets an offline player.

The chancery view

Every site’s health and use, on one page, behind a passcode

All 73 parishes by vicariate: which are on the platform, which have a website, which have Mass times on the finder. For each parish on the platform, what its office has confirmed and what is still waiting, what its calendar holds for the next month, pages served, where finder searches come from and which came up empty, forms sent and Give taps. Counts only: no cookies, no visitor ids, no addresses, no contents of any form, nothing about any person. It is built, it is part of the license, and we will open it on the walkthrough.

Section eight

A parish site that is alive.

Under the finder and the chancery’s view sits what each parish gets. On the platform a parish’s schedule, calendar and week’s news are the same data, so the site moves when the parish does. The next Mass is worked out from the clock. A holy day shows on every page the week before. The week’s news is typed once and prints as the bulletin insert, and the Watch page knows when Mass is. Nobody at the parish logs into anything: the office texts or emails a change and it is up the same week.

No Lansing parish has been built yet; that is the first call’s decision, not ours to make for a parish. Three complete parish sites run on the platform in the Diocese of Kalamazoo, and they are what a Lansing parish would get: St. Mary Marshall ↗, St. Philip Battle Creek ↗ (78 pages read in), and San Felipe de Jesús ↗, in Spanish throughout. Each has its Mass times as data, the next Mass from the clock, a calendar anyone can subscribe to, three forms that reach the office, religious education enrollment, the sacraments as checklists, a news page that prints as the bulletin insert, and the diocese layer at its foot.

A first Lansing parish is a day of reading its current site into the platform’s data, a person reading the result once, and the office confirming the handful of facts only it knows. Name as many as you like and we build them at no charge, as the three Kalamazoo sites were built.
