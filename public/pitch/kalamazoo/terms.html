The terms, in plain words

What the Diocese of Kalamazoo would be agreeing to.

This is the text of the agreement we would put in front of the diocese, as it stands on November 5, 2026: Exhibit A: Diocese of Kalamazoo, prepared 2026-11-02. It sits on top of the glazedweb Client Agreement v1.1 ↗, the general terms every Glazed Web client gets. Where the two differ, this page governs, and part 3 says where.

Opening this page signs nothing. The copy to sign comes after the call, with the diocese’s legal name on it.

Part 1

What is included.

  • One codebase, every parish on its own domain with its own photos, words, palette, schedule and calendar. The diocese holds its data, its content, its domains and its accounts outright; the platform is glazedweb’s, and part 3 sets out the copy of it and the license the diocese holds.
  • For each parish that joins: home, Mass times and directions, New here?, about (contact, staff, history), sacraments, faith formation, ministries, calendar, bulletin where the parish prints one, give, register, Mass intentions and prayer requests, in English or in Spanish throughout.
  • Mass, Confession and Adoration times with the next one computed from the clock, the parish calendar as the parish’s own data with a feed anyone can subscribe to, every photo and document carried over from the old site as the parish’s own files, and three forms that email the parish office.
  • Every parish’s schedule published as structured data on its own site and joined into one diocesan feed: name, address, language, Mass, Confession and Adoration times, for the Parish Finder, for Google and for the Mass-finder apps to read from the diocese.
  • The diocese layer, on the diocese’s own address: Mass near you (a zip, a town or the phone’s location; every parish within a radius, nearest first, with its next Mass from the clock), covering all sixty parishes and missions from the first day, the three on the platform from their own data and the rest read from the diocesan Parish Finder, each marked with its source; this week across the diocese (every platform parish’s calendar on one page, by kind and town, with add-to-calendar links); the groups across the diocese with their contacts; every parish on one page; and the chancery’s notices, written once and shown on every parish site that has not turned them off. Each parish site carries its three nearest parishes and their next Mass on its Mass times page. The finder runs on its own address from the first day, re-reads the diocesan Parish Finder weekly, says on every card when it last did, carries a wrong-time report that reaches the chancery, and may be embedded on the diocese’s own site, so the diocese can make it its finder before any parish moves.
  • The bishop’s voice: recordings the diocese has published (messages, homilies, talks), listed once by the diocese and carried on every parish home page that has not turned them off, in the parish’s language where the recording exists in it, with a feed anyone can subscribe to without a social network. A Watch page on every parish that streams, which shows the parish’s own YouTube or Facebook stream from a quarter hour before Mass to ninety minutes after and the next Mass in words otherwise. A line at the top of every parish page in the week before a holy day or a special Mass, with the times, from the same data as the schedule.
  • The migration of each parish: its eCatholic pages read into the platform’s data by script, read once by a person, and the handful of facts only the office knows confirmed with the office before the site goes live on its own domain. The old subscription ends after that, never before.
  • In the order the diocese sets. Unless it says otherwise: the three sites already built (St. Mary Marshall, St. Philip Battle Creek, San Felipe de Jesús Fennville), then the parishes and missions with no website at all, then the parishes the diocese picks, then the rest as each one’s eCatholic agreement allows.
  • What a parish already has stays: giving on its own provider (the Give button links straight to it), the bulletin where it is printed and hosted, Flocknote, a YouTube or Facebook livestream linked as Watch Mass online, and its church-management system. The platform links to them and replaces none of them.
  • Search for every page: a title and description of its own, a Church record with the Mass schedule, a sitemap, a link card so a shared link shows the church. The small studio credit in each footer.
  • The diocese’s own website, if the diocese wants it on the platform: the pages its current site’s menu links to, read into the platform’s data with their text, people, documents and pictures, with the finder, this week across the parishes and the bishop’s voice built into its home page and a staff directory built from the office pages. A record that must exist in one place only (the list of those disqualified from ministry) is never copied; its page points to where the diocese keeps it. The diocese may keep its current site instead, and the finder can be embedded there.
  • The chancery view, behind a passcode the diocese holds: every site’s health and use on one page, by deanery. What each parish’s office has confirmed and what is still waiting, what each calendar holds for the next two months, pages served, where finder searches come from and which came up empty, forms sent and Give taps. Counts only, with no cookies, no visitor ids, no addresses and no contents of any form.
  • The week’s news written once: a news page on every parish compiled from its schedule, calendar, the chancery’s notices and the bishop’s message plus anything the office types with the dates it should run; the same rows as a feed any email tool can send and as a bulletin insert laid out to print; and for the diocese, this week across all parishes on one page that is also a feed, for the communications office’s weekly email. Mass intention requests that offer the parish’s actual Masses for the coming six weeks.

Not included, and quoted separately if wanted

An office panel for self-service edits, a diocesan directory, and any other work beyond keeping the sites current, each of which is quoted first and approved before it starts; online giving changes of any kind, which stay with each parish’s provider; eCatholic’s giving, messaging and church-management products, which this agreement does not replace; the add-ons a parish may choose and pays for itself (hosting its livestream on its own player with automatic recordings and clips, $89 a month; an office panel for self-service edits, $29 a month; texting at cost plus a margin), none of which changes the diocesan license; photography; printed materials; and a parish that comes outside this agreement, which pays the published build price under its own agreement.

No parish is billed. Every parish site in the diocese, however many, with hosting, SSL, security updates, backups and the checks run again after every change; the diocesan feed and the diocese layer (Mass near you on its own address, this week across the diocese, the groups, the notices, the bishop’s voice, the holy-day line, a Watch page for every parish that streams, the chancery view); and the edits each parish needs through the year. A twelve-month first term from the first piece going live, then month to month; part 3 says how.
Included editsUp to 2 hours per month for each parish of small edits: a Mass time, a holy day schedule, a new pastor, a ministry contact, a document swapped for a new one, a line of copy, the calendar for a new season. The office emails or texts the change and it is up the same week.
Beyond scope$125 per hour, always quoted and approved in writing before any work starts. Nothing lands on a bill unannounced.
The domainsEach parish’s own domain, each in the parish’s or the diocese’s own registrar account.
Part 3

The diocesan agreement and its first term.

This agreement is between glazedweb and the diocese, for every parish the diocese brings onto the platform. These terms say how a parish joins, how long the first term is, and what happens after it. They are part of the agreement, and where they and section 5 of the general terms differ on how long the agreement lasts, these govern. Section 3 of the general terms (“You own the website”) was written for one site built for a fee; on this agreement, what the diocese owns, and the copy of the platform and the license it holds, are set out below, and where they and section 3 differ, these govern.

  1. The diocese signs once. One acceptance covers every parish the diocese brings on. A parish joins by a note from the Pastoral Center naming the parish, its domain and the office that will confirm its facts. No parish signs anything, and no parish pays a build fee under this agreement. A parish or mission with no website today joins the same way.
  2. The first term is twelve months. The agreement runs twelve months from the day the first of those is live on an address the diocese or a parish owns: the diocese’s own site, the diocese layer, or a parish site. During those twelve months neither of us can end it except for a material breach, on the fifteen days’ written notice and chance to fix the problem that the general terms give. The twelve months stand in for a build fee: every parish is migrated at no charge against them. After the first term the agreement is month to month, and either of us can end it on thirty days’ written notice with no fee, as the general terms say.
  3. What is billed, and to whom. One diocesan license, $3,990 a month, billed to the diocese from the first day of the month after the first of these is live on an address the diocese or a parish owns: the diocese’s own site, the diocese layer, or a parish site. It covers every parish and mission in the diocese, however many move and whenever they do, so no parish is ever billed for its site and the number does not change when a parish joins. The diocese may recharge its parishes as it sees fit; that is between the diocese and its parishes. A parish that comes onto the platform outside this agreement pays the published build price and $99 a month under its own agreement.
  4. Leaving, and what the diocese keeps. Ending the agreement ends it for every parish on it. The diocese and each parish keep everything that is theirs under “Export at any time”: every page as readable files and as a static site that runs on any host, every photo and document, every schedule, calendar, event, group, notice and form submission as data, their domains in their own registrar accounts, and their Stripe accounts with every donor and recurring gift in them. Export stays open for ninety days after the ending; glazedweb then deletes what it holds and confirms it in writing. A parish the diocese releases from the agreement can continue on its own at the same $99 a month, month to month, with no build fee, under its own agreement.
  5. Support and uptime. Every parish site and the diocese layer are available 99.9% of each calendar month, measured from outside by a monitor whose record the diocese may ask for at any time. A wrong Mass time, a cancelled Mass, a funeral notice or a site that is down is acted on the same day, seven days a week. Every other edit is up the same week. A security report is answered the same day.
  6. Continuity. The diocese holds a copy of the platform and a license to run it. Each quarter glazedweb delivers an encrypted copy of the platform’s source code and build documentation to storage the diocese names, with its hash and the exact version attested in writing; the key is held by glazedweb’s attorney. The diocese’s license to that copy is perpetual, non-exclusive and royalty-free: to host its own parishes, schools and institutions, to modify the code, and to hire anyone it chooses to do so; not to resell it or to host it for another diocese. The license takes effect, and the key is released, if glazedweb dissolves, gives written notice that it is ceasing operations, files for bankruptcy or insolvency, discontinues the platform, terminates this agreement for convenience, or does not cure a material breach of “Support and uptime” within sixty days of written notice. This agreement is a license of intellectual property under 11 U.S.C. § 365(n), and those copies are embodiments of it. The diocese’s own decision to end the agreement does not bring the license into effect; what it keeps then is set out under “Leaving”.
  7. Reading the diocese’s own pages. The diocese asks us to read its own public Parish Finder and its parishes’ public websites, for the finder and for each parish’s migration, and the result belongs to the diocese. A demonstration site built from a parish’s public pages stays hidden from search engines, goes live at that parish’s address only with the parish’s yes, and comes down the day the parish asks.
  8. What is stored, and what is not. The platform keeps no card data, no visitor accounts and no member database; giving stays on each parish’s own provider and the forms go to the parish office. What is stored, what is not and where it runs is published at /diocese/security on the platform and changes with the code. A security report is answered the same day.
  9. The feed is the diocese’s data. The diocesan feed, every parish’s name, address, language, Mass, Confession and Adoration times as one file, is the diocese’s content under section 3. It is published at an address the diocese names, in the shape its Parish Finder reads, and kept valid as schedules change. It depends on nothing of eCatholic’s, so the Finder keeps reading it as parishes leave eCatholic one at a time.
  10. Export at any time. At any time, for any reason, with no fee, the diocese or any parish on the agreement can have every page as readable files, every photo and document, every schedule, calendar, event, group, notice and form submission as data, and a static copy of each site that runs on any host, within ten days of asking. Export is self-serve where the platform offers it and on written request otherwise, and it does not end the agreement.
  11. The data has a way out while it is in. Every parish’s schedule, calendar and events are published as feeds (structured data, iCalendar and JSON) that anyone the diocese chooses may read, and the diocese may build on them without asking. We do not block, rate-limit or charge for reading the diocese’s own data.
  12. Giving, stated plainly. A parish that takes gifts or payments through the platform does so on a Stripe account in the parish’s own name at Stripe’s nonprofit rate (2.2% plus 30 cents a card gift at the time of writing), so its donors and their recurring gifts belong to the parish and travel with it. The platform’s fee is 1% of each gift, capped at $5 a gift, which the giver is offered to cover at the time of giving and which the parish pays when the giver does not. There is no monthly fee for giving. The diocese may run its annual appeal on the same terms on an account in its own name. A parish that keeps its current giving provider keeps it, and the Give button on its site goes there.
