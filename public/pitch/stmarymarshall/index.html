Section one

What we found.

None of this is a complaint about the parish. The current site has what matters most on its front page, Mass times, the address and the phone, which is more than many parish sites manage. What follows is about the details a visitor meets after that, read in a browser on October 27, 2026. Tap the link under each one to see it yourself.

Three pages are titled “Please click to learn more…”

The About page’s title, the words a search engine shows and the browser tab carries, is “Please click to learn more about the community of St. Mary.” Faith Formation and Councils/Ministries are titled the same way. The instruction meant for a menu became the name of the page, so someone searching “St. Mary Marshall staff” sees a line that tells them to click rather than what they will find. The About, Sacraments and Ministries pages carry no description at all for search engines, and the one on the home page is a list of keywords with “RCIA” in it twice.

The history ends two bishops ago

The parish history says “Bishop Paul Bradley is the current Bishop.” Bishop Edward M. Lohse was installed as the fifth Bishop of Kalamazoo on July 25, 2023, as the diocese and St. Philip in Battle Creek both say. A small thing, and the kind of small thing a visitor from another parish notices first. The new site’s history has been brought up to date.

The We Care Fund has two phone numbers

The Donate page says to reach Gus Groat at 269-781-0008. The Knights of Columbus page says 269-830-7744. One of them is current and a family in need will dial the other. The new site keeps both where they were and asks the office which is right, because that is a question only the office can answer.

Nothing on the site says when the office is open

The phone number is everywhere; the hours are nowhere. A visitor who wants to drop off a registration form, pay a stipend or ask about a baptism has to call to find out when calling works. St. Philip in Battle Creek publishes its office hours on the front page. The new site has a place for them and is waiting on the answer.

The home page loads 208 images, seven of them 1,920 pixels wide, on every phone

Measured in a browser on the home page: 19 scripts, 8 stylesheets and 208 image files, including all seven slideshow photographs at full size whether or not the visitor’s phone can show them. Two of the slides are a “Thank You For Your Support” card and a parking-lot notice about work that finished in early September. Sharing the site’s link in a text or on Facebook shows no picture, because no page declares one.

The parish’s files are on somebody else’s server

The registration form, the cemetery map, the cemetery rules and fees, the Religious Education calendars, and Fr. Craig’s Christmas letter and mid-summer update are all served from files.ecatholic.com/6225/…. So are the seven slideshow photographs. The calendar is an eCatholic feed. The day the eCatholic subscription ends, every one of those links stops working, and there is no copy of them on anything the parish holds. The new site carries all of them as the parish’s own files, on the parish’s own address.

Small things a parishioner noticed

The About page has a MyParishApp tab with nothing under it. The email address on the home page is plain text, so a phone cannot tap it. The “Sync to my Calendar” button works only while eCatholic does. None of these takes an afternoon to fix; all of them are fixed on the new site.

A parish office has a hundred things to do before the website, and the current site was the right call when it was made. These are the details a visitor meets, and details are what I do for a living. I would rather fix them for my own parish than point at them.

What I would do instead

The parish owns it. All of it.

The site becomes the parish’s in writing on day one, since there is no build fee to wait for: the design, the source code, every photo and PDF, the calendar data. If the parish ever leaves me, it gets the working project, repository, hosting and logins. Not a snapshot. Not an export. Month to month, thirty days’ notice, no exit fee. That clause is titled No hostage-taking, because I would rather say it plainly than bury it.

Two questions worth asking the office this week

Who holds the eCatholic login, and the domain login?The domain is the parish’s most valuable piece of the internet. Make sure more than one person can get into the registrar, and that the renewal card is not an old one.
Which inbox should the website write to?Registrations, Mass intentions and prayer requests need an address a person opens every day. If that is stmarymarshall@aol.com, good; if not, the office knows which.

Section five

What it costs.

The published menu price for a site this size: $2,000
A gift
The build. Fourteen pages, three forms, the calendar, the parish’s files brought home. It is already done.
Same menu, ongoing
$150/mo
Hosting, security updates, backups, and the edits: holy day times, a new staff member, a changed Mass time, the calendar for next year. Text or email the change; it is up the same week.

The struck number is Glazed Web’s own published price for a six-page site with forms, the Baker’s Dozen on glazedweb.com ↗. The monthly is the same menu’s monthly; the parish is not being charged a special rate, only spared the build.

That is $1,800 a year, replacing the eCatholic subscription. I have not seen that invoice and will not guess at it; the office has the number. Work beyond keeping the site current, a new section or a new tool, is quoted first and approved before it starts.

Why the monthly is not zero

A website with nobody paid to mind it stops being minded. Dates age, and the person who built it as a favor has a job. The monthly is what makes “can you change the Christmas Mass times” a normal request to a vendor rather than a favor to ask of a parishioner, and it is the same arrangement every other client of mine has, in writing.
